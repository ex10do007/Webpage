Are we solving fragmentation with AI agents, or creating a new kind of fragmentation?

20 November 2026

Most organizations already operate with a complex landscape of technologies and systems.

Employees spend significant effort on what I call “bridge work”: moving information between systems, switching contexts, coordinating handoffs, and stitching together an end-to-end process.

Now AI agents are entering this landscape.

They can certainly accelerate individual tasks. But there is also a risk:

If every new agent becomes another standalone component that people need to connect, coordinate and manage, are we really reducing work, or simply creating more bridge work?

This made me think that the future digital workplace needs something beyond individual agents.

It needs a Unified AI Operating System sitting above applications, systems and agents, orchestrating them into simple end-to-end workflows.

The objective should not be more agents. It should be less friction between everything that gets work done.

Such an AI operating system could bring together:

  • Enterprise applications and data
  • Specialized AI agents
  • Internal and external digital workers
  • Human experts and fractional professionals
  • Governance, permissions and human approvals

All orchestrated around the outcome we want to achieve, rather than the systems we need to navigate.

Perhaps the future workplace is not about giving every employee dozens of AI agents. It is about making those agents invisible behind one coherent way of getting work done.
